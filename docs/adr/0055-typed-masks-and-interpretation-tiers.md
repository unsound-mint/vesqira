# ADR 0055: Typed masks and interpretation tiers

Status: **proposed; implemented, awaiting owner review.** Decided by the implementing agent under the owner's delegation of ADR decisions (roadmap Stage 5; Q201, Q211). The interface additions (a Masks section, policy controls, hatch overlays, tier badges) sit inside existing surfaces and have **not** had design review or approval (CLAUDE.md design gate).

## Context
AGENTS.md invariant 5 says missing values, invalid pixels, low-SNR flags, saturated measurements and user exclusions have distinct meanings, and that zero is never missing. Until now only one of them existed: NaN, BLANK and infinite values are counted as "no value" by the viewer and every analysis, and that was the whole vocabulary. A researcher could not say "leave out the saturated pixels in this statistic and keep the low-S/N ones", and nothing recorded what had been left out. Separately, interpretations carried only `declaredBy` (`user` or an adapter's name): a researcher's word, a rule's proposal and a check the app ran were not told apart (invariant 7, Q211).

## Decision
**Masks**
- **A mask is a derived resource with a type.** `missing` (no value in the file: NaN, BLANK, infinite; *computed from the data, never declared*), `invalid`, `low_snr`, `saturated`, `user_excluded`, and `other` (a description is required). A mask flags pixels for one stated reason; no type means "good" or "bad" in general. Zero is a value: it is flagged by no mask unless the stated rule says so (a threshold above zero never does; "value / σ < k" does, by its rule, for a measured zero over a known σ, and not as "missing"). The worker reuses `catalog_table.snr_filter`: only the status `below` is low S/N. A pixel with no value, or whose σ is unknown, zero or negative, is not low S/N; it is counted in the rule (`sigma_unknown_pixels`).
- **One uint8 FITS file per mask** (0 not flagged, 1 flagged), in the derived folder as `mask-<dataset>-<type>-<random>.fits`, never inside the data file and never altering it. A mask has either one plane `(ny, nx)` that applies to every channel (regions, most imports) or one plane per channel `(channels, ny, nx)` whose axes are channel, y, x whatever the data's native order (stated in `VESQMAXS`; the spatial and spectral WCS cards are copied). Header cards say the type, the method and the data file's name; the rule is in HISTORY and in the row. *Why not bit planes in one file:* each mask has its own author, rule, fingerprint and lifetime; bit planes would cap the count at eight, couple the types' lifecycles and mean rewriting a file that results already name to add a type.
- **A side table, not a dataset.** `masks` (schema 18) holds: the dataset, type, name, the file, shape, flagged and total pixel counts, tier and mapping version, method (`computed`, `regions`, `import`), the rule as the worker described it (JSON: estimator, threshold, where σ or the threshold came from, regions, file), a free meaning, author, time, **what it was made from** (the dataset's size and modification time, and its recorded content identity, `sha256:<hex>` or `sampled:<fingerprint>` or empty, ADR 0053) and **its own identity** (size, mtime and SHA-256 of the mask file). A mask is not registered as a dataset so it does not clutter the explorer, and it has no `derived` record of its own: its row is its provenance.
- **Making masks** (`POST /api/datasets/{id}/masks`): `missing` (computed); `low_snr` (k and σ from a stated value, a robust estimate named in the rule: 1.4826 × the median absolute deviation of each channel about its median, which includes the signal and says so, or a declared noise map on the same pixels); `saturated` (a threshold, *at or above*, and a required source for it); `regions` (the union of saved rectangles and circles by the same pixel-centre rule the region spectrum and statistics use, `Cube.region_mask`; a line is refused; the mask applies to every channel); `import` (a registered image of the same shape, with a declared type, what a nonzero pixel means (`flagged` or `valid`, required) and a description for `other`; a file with NaN is refused, since NaN is neither flagged nor not). Two files whose WCS cannot confirm that they share a grid are matched by pixel shape only, and the rule says so; a declared axis that differs is refused. A file on a host gets its mask on that host, written in the host's derived folder by the host's copy of the program, with the noise map or mask file on the same machine (ADR 0064). A map declared as an axial orientation map refuses a S/N or saturation threshold (its values are angles).
- **Applying masks is the operation's decision, per mask, and has no default.** A request names each mask with `exclude`, `keep` or `weight` (a factor 0 to 1). Masks not named are not applied. The policy for `missing` is `exclude` only (a pixel with no value cannot be kept or weighted). `weight` is accepted only where the analysis already has weights: relative orientation between two maps (the factor multiplies the declared reliability). Statistics and moment maps take exclude and keep; weight is refused with a sentence. Implemented in statistics, moment maps and map-against-map relative orientation (masks of either map), and in the region spectrum and the region histogram (below). A mask must belong to the dataset it is applied to, be **fresh** (made from the bytes the dataset has now) and its file unchanged; a stale mask is refused (409) and shown as stale.
- **No-value pixels stay counted apart.** `no_value` counts pixels with no value in the file whatever mask is applied; `excluded_by_mask` counts pixels *with a value* that a mask left out. Excluding a mask never turns anything into zero.
- **Outputs carry what was applied.** The derived record of a moment map or relative-orientation result has, in its summary, every mask applied with its id, name, type, policy, factor, tier, fingerprint and the pixels flagged and excluded; in its parameters, an **id-free snapshot** (type, name, policy, factor, tier, mapping version, method, rule, fingerprint, flagged pixels), which is what the reviewer-safe provenance shows and what survives forgetting the mask or deleting its dataset. A statistics result returns the same list in its summary. The moment map's file says in HISTORY which masks were applied.
- **Viewer.** A mask is shown as a hatch over the plane, one pattern per type (dots, diagonals `/` and `\`, cross, horizontal, vertical) with a legend that names the type, the mask and its pixels, so types are told apart without colour. Pixels with no value keep the viewer's own hatch. The overlay is a view setting of the moment; a figure saved while masks are shown draws them and records them (below), and a figure of a result carries the masks applied *to it* and those carried into it through its provenance.
- **Region spectrum and region histogram take masks (implemented).** The same per-mask policy as statistics: `GET /api/datasets/{id}/spectrum` and the new `GET /api/datasets/{id}/region-histogram` accept `mask=<id>:<policy>` (repeated, at most 8); a mask not named is not applied, `missing` is exclude-only, `weight` is refused with a sentence, and a mask of another dataset (422) or a stale one (409) is refused by the same `maskSpecs`/`usableMask` the analyses use (for a file on a host the host's worker applies the masks kept next to it, ADR 0064). The browser has no mask bytes, so a masked request is computed by the plane worker (`Cube.region_masks`, reading the masks through `masks.Applied`, with a region window so the counts are the region's own); the unmasked spectrum and the unmasked region histogram are unchanged (the histogram is still counted in the browser from the plane it holds, and the request body of an unmasked spectrum is byte-for-byte the old one). The histogram has its own route because it is a one-channel count the browser otherwise does itself; the worker bins by the browser's rule (equal steps from the plane's min to max, the largest value in the last bin), so a masked and an unmasked histogram share their axis. Results carry `no_value` (before any mask) apart from `excluded_by_mask`, and `masks`: each with id, name, type, policy, tier, fingerprint and the pixels flagged with a value and left out, summed over the channels read like statistics. The cache key includes each mask's fingerprint and policy. The choice is made once in the Region panel (one `MaskPolicies` control per mask, none applied until chosen) and applies to the region's spectrum and to its histogram; it is a view setting that starts empty with every dataset. A spectrum or histogram with masks is not a saved figure input: the figure's scene does not record the choice yet (specified, not built).
- **Cutout, slab, virtual subset, align and convolve carry masks (implemented, schema unchanged).** The researcher chooses, per mask, to carry it or leave it behind: `carryMasks: [{id, carry}]` on `POST /api/analysis` (cutout, slab, align, convolve) and on `POST /api/subsets/{id}/materialise`. **Nothing is carried by default.** A cut leaves behind a mask that is not listed; align and convolve refuse (422) until *every* mask of the file is decided, so no choice is made silently. A mask chosen for carrying goes through the same checks as an applied one (`usableMask`: the dataset's own, fresh, its file unchanged), so a stale mask is refused (409) and can only be left behind. The worker carries inside the same invocation as the data, so the window, grid or kernel are the very objects the data went through (`masks.carry_after`, reading each mask through the same `open_mask` that `Applied` uses). Each carried mask is **a new mask of the result**: the same type and name, the **tier and mapping version unchanged**, method `carried`, a rule that says `carried from mask "<name>" of <source> by <operation and window>` (with the source mask's own rule, tier and fingerprint nested under `carriedFrom`, and `flagsFollow` stating how a flag follows the data), made fresh against the result (its source bytes are the result's), and the fingerprint (SHA-256) of its own file; the carried copy has Author `user`. The rules by which a flag follows the data:
  - *Cutout, slab, subset:* the same slice the data was cut with (a box, a channel range, a window with its step); nothing else is flagged and nothing is resampled. A per-channel mask follows the channel range; a one-plane mask applies to every channel and is kept whole by a slab. A subset validates a mask against the *source's* shape and cuts it by the resolved window, step and channel range.
  - *Align (reprojection):* the mask is reprojected as a float plane with `reproject_exact` (area overlap) onto the reference grid, whatever method the data used, and an output pixel is **flagged where more than a millionth of the input area it covers is flagged**: any contributing flagged pixel flags it, and a pixel outside the input's footprint (NaN) is not flagged. Nearest-neighbour on the mask was rejected: with a bilinear or exact data method it drops contributors. The one-millionth floor is arithmetic, not a choice about contribution: the polygon clipping makes an edge-touching neighbour come out at about 1e-10 rather than 0. The rule names the reference file.
  - *Convolve:* an output pixel is flagged when the kernel's **truncation square**, the square of half-width `kernel_half_width_pixels` (4 sigma of its wider axis) that the data was convolved with, centred on it holds a flagged pixel. This is the footprint as applied, not a smaller ellipse chosen by hand; it is conservative, grows the flagged area by the half-width in every direction, and a beam already reached (no kernel) leaves the mask as it is.
  - *The `missing` mask is never carried.* When the source has one, the result gets a new `missing` mask **computed from the result's own values** (verified, method computed; a cut, an interpolation and a convolution all change which pixels have a value). The no-value mask cannot be chosen (422), and the forms say so.
  - *Zero is never flagged by carrying*: carrying reads the mask and not the data, so a value of zero is flagged only if the source mask flagged that pixel.
  What was **left behind** is recorded too: the result's derived record keeps, under `maskCarry` (not `masks`, which means *applied*), the masks carried (source and carried fingerprints, tier, rule), those left behind (type, name, tier, fingerprint), and `recomputed: ["missing"]`, all id-free. The reviewer's provenance has a tier line for each: `mask carried into this result`, `mask left behind` and `mask: no value (computed again)`. A virtual subset written as a file is reused only for the same carry choice (the reuse key includes the carried masks and their fingerprints). Carrying into a result written on a host (a host job, or a subset written there, ADR 0064) is refused with the way out: leave the masks behind and make them again on the result, on the host; a subset file on a host records them as left behind. Not built: carrying masks through the other operations (moment maps and differences produce a value per pixel from many, which has no stated flag rule yet), and carrying a mask to a result on a host.
- **A saved figure draws and records the masks shown (implemented).** When a figure is saved from a view with masks shown, the picture draws their hatch (the same six patterns, in the figure's ink, clipped to the plane) and a legend of one line per mask in words (type, name, tier, pixels flagged), in the PNG and the SVG alike (one layout, `layoutImageFigure`, and one list of masks on `ImageFigure`). A mask drawn in blocks, because it has more runs than the viewer will draw exactly, says so in its legend line. The scene (`scene/1`, optional `masks`, at most 8) records each one by `id`, `type`, `name`, `tier` and `fingerprint`; the server **overwrites type, name, tier and fingerprint from its own row** and refuses a mask of another dataset (422), a stale one or one whose file changed (409), and a fingerprint that is not the row's (409). The reviewer sees only the provenance, with a tier line `mask shown: <type> (<name>)` carrying the tier, the pixels flagged, the rule and the fingerprint, never an id or a path; the scene is owner-side. **Draw again** (ADR 0042, section 5) compares each recorded mask with the catalog (`GET /api/figures/{id}/redraw-check` gains `masks`: `unchanged`, `changed` (another fingerprint, other bytes than the dataset has now, or its file changed) or `forgotten`, by type, name and tier) and a changed or forgotten one moves the state to `ask`, said before anything is drawn. The redrawing hatches the unchanged masks, leaves the others out with a note per mask, and the revision it saves records what its picture shows. Not built: a masked region spectrum or histogram as a figure input, and a mask on a channel-map grid figure or a spectrum figure.
- **Lifecycle.** Masks go with their dataset when it is deleted (impact group `masks: removed`, part of the delete digest); the mask files on disk are never deleted (ADR 0041). Results made with them keep their own snapshot. Forgetting a mask (`POST /api/masks/{id}/delete`) keeps the file as well.

**Tiers**
- `verified` (established by a check the app ran: the file's BUNIT present and read; a `missing` mask computed from the data), `declared` (the researcher), `inferred` (proposed by an adapter or rule, named by `declaredBy`, with a **mapping version** such as `vgt-run-adapter/1`) and `unresolved`. They are the app's words; the worker's header fields keep theirs (`bunit_status: "declared"` still means *the file says it*). *Verified is never the default and never a claim that the science is right*: reading BUNIT is not checking it against the data, and the provenance says so. No orientation tier can be `verified` yet: no provider to check angles against exists.
- `tier` and `mapping_version` are columns of `interpretations`, `dataset_units` and `catalog_declarations`; a mask has its own. Written from the author: `user` is `declared`; anything else is `inferred` with the adapter's mapping version (`catalog.adapterMappings`, all `1` today; a changed mapping gets a new version there). Confirming a proposal makes it the researcher's: declared, no mapping version.
- **Migration (schema 18), faithful:** `declared_by = 'user'` becomes `declared`; every other value (`vgt-run-adapter`, `rohsa-run-adapter`, `vesqira-analysis`, anything) becomes `inferred` with the rule left in `declared_by` and mapping version `"1"`, because versions did not exist and every such row was made by the first mapping. Nothing migrates to `verified`: no existing row was checked.
- **Shown wherever a declaration is shown:** the inspector's unit rows (a new "Unit tier" row), the unit control, the orientation panel, the compare inspector, the catalog declaration, masks (list and legend), statistics and "Made from" (the masks applied), and in figure provenance a `tiers` list (brightness unit, orientation, each mask applied) rendered by the review page, the print view and the presentation view. A reviewer sees tiers, rules and fingerprints, never ids or paths.

## Alternatives
- **Bit planes in one mask file:** rejected above. **A mask as a registered dataset:** free `derived` and fingerprint plumbing, but it would fill the explorer with uint8 images, and datasets are things a researcher works on; the row is the provenance here. Revisit if masks should be shared between datasets.
- **A global or default policy ("always exclude low S/N"):** rejected: it is the silent merge of meanings invariant 5 forbids; each analysis states it per mask.
- **Weights for statistics and moments:** not invented: a weighted moment or standard deviation needs a stated definition. Relative orientation already has weights.
- **A fifth tier for "checked by a provider":** not needed until a provider exists.

## Consequences
- Statistics, moment maps and relative orientation of two maps accept masks; statistics and moment maps run as jobs on a host take them too (ADR 0064); the comparison of a map with a catalog does not (409/422 with a sentence). Cutout, slab, subset, align and convolve carry the masks the researcher chooses, and a saved figure draws and records the masks shown (above). **Specified, not built:** a mask carried through moment maps and differences (no stated flag rule), masks viewed on a virtual subset (they are in the source's pixels; write the subset as a file with its masks), a masked region spectrum or histogram as a figure input, and a mask editor beyond regions, import and rules. The new forms (the carry choice in the analysis and subset panels, the hatch and legend on a figure) have not had design review.
- A mask is a file next to the derived outputs: backup and export (ADR 0022) treat it like them (not verified here).
- `GET /api/datasets/{id}` and the figure provenance gain `tier`, `mappingVersion` and `tiers`; clients that ignore them keep working. A figure saved before has no `tiers`.
- The schema number: main was 17 (VPN companion) when this landed; 18 here. The contiguity test enforces 2..Version and a snapshot per version.

## Rollback / Migration
Carrying masks and figure masks add no schema (a new `method` value, `carried`, and JSON in existing columns). Upgrading creates `masks` and the three pairs of columns and stamps schema 18 after the usual copy to `<db>.before-schema-18-<time>`. An older build refuses a version 18 catalog; going back means restoring that copy (masks made since are lost from the catalog; their files stay on disk) or, by hand, dropping the `masks` table and setting `PRAGMA user_version = 17` (the extra columns are ignored by the older code, which reads columns by name; declaration tiers then read as before from `declared_by`).
