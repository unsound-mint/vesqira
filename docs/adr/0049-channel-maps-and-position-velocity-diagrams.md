# ADR 0049: Channel maps and position-velocity diagrams

- Status: proposed; implemented, awaiting owner review
- Date: 2026-11-11
- Related: ADR 0035 (tiles and preview), 0040 (readout conversions), 0042 (scene, figures), 0026 (regions), roadmap Stage S ("Channel-map grid and position-velocity diagram along a line")

## Context
Two standard ways of reading a spectral cube were missing: a grid of channel maps (the same sky at consecutive velocities, side by side) and a position-velocity (PV) diagram (velocity against position along a cut). Both are views of one cube that depend on the spectral axis, so they inherit its risks: a velocity needs a rest frequency and a declared convention (ADR 0040), averaging channels must not hide missing values, and a cut that interpolates is not a measurement of one pixel.

## Decision

### 1. Channel-map grid
- **Panels.** A range of channels (first, last), a stride (every k-th channel) and an average (a panel is the mean of `average` channels, not more than the stride, so panels never share a channel). At most 64 panels. A panel that is the mean of several channels is labelled "Mean of channels a–b" with the spectral coordinate of its first and last channel.
- **One scale.** All panels use one colour scale, one stretch and one colour table, stated under the grid. The limits are numbers the worker computes once (`channel_grid`): the 1st to 99th percentile of the finite pixels pooled over up to six evenly spaced panels, at the preview resolution; or custom limits the person types. They are never recomputed per panel and never from whichever panels happened to load, because panels read lazily. The caption says which panels the scale was sampled from.
- **Labels.** The file's own axis unit as given (no silent km/s or MHz), or a unit chosen from the readout conversions (ADR 0040) with the velocity convention the file declares or the person chose, the rest frequency card and SPECSYS (not changed). The labels go through the same `convert.spectral_plan` as the readout (refactored from `convert_spectral`, which now calls it), so a label and a readout cannot disagree; a test compares them. A refused conversion leaves the native labels and says why, and the grid still draws. No rest frequency is invented.
- **Reading panels.** The plan is JSON (`GET /api/datasets/{id}/channel-grid`). Each panel is read as the viewer reads a plane: one channel by the plane route, a mean by `GET /slab` (interactive lane). A panel loads when it scrolls into view and "Load all" reads the rest. Caps: panels × pixels per panel × 4 bytes at most 64 MiB in all and 1 Mpixel per panel; a larger plane is reduced by a block mean (the step is chosen by the worker, stated as a preview, and `step` goes into the stored description). For a mean the order is stated and fixed: the per-pixel mean of the finite values of the panel's channels at full resolution, then the block mean.
- **NaN.** The slab mean excludes non-finite values and counts them; a pixel with no finite value is NaN (hatched, not a colour of the scale). Flagged channels keep their data and a panel says that one of its channels was flagged.
- **Overlays.** Overlays of the base scene are not drawn on the panels, and the caption says so ("The map's n overlays are not drawn"). Drawing contours per panel needs the overlay geometry of every panel's channel; that is not cheap and is deferred.
- **Orientation maps.** Refused with 409 (a mean over channels of axial angles is not defined; invariant 4).

### 2. Position-velocity diagram
- **Path and width.** A polyline of whole-pixel vertices (2 to 64) and a width across it in pixels or arcsec. The worker samples the path at spacing of at most one pixel (arc length in pixels, `ceil(length)+1` samples, at most 16 384) and takes `round(width)` samples across at one-pixel spacing, perpendicular to the segment each sample lies on, and averages the finite ones; the count of finite samples is returned for every cell. An arcsec width becomes pixels with the local pixel scale at the path's midpoint (astropy separations), and the note says so. A file without a celestial WCS gives offsets in pixels and refuses an arcsec width.
- **Sampling is labelled.** `linear` is bilinear interpolation from the four surrounding pixels, written in numpy (vectorised over a block of channels) and checked against `scipy.ndimage.map_coordinates(order=1)` in the tests. A sample is excluded when a neighbour that has weight is not finite (it is not treated as zero or silently renormalised). `nearest` takes the nearest pixel and no interpolation. The diagram says which, in words. scipy is not a runtime dependency.
- **Offsets.** The offset of each sample is the cumulative great-circle separation (`SkyCoord.separation`, astropy) of the sky positions of the samples along the centre line, in arcsec, from the first vertex, positive along the path. It is returned as an explicit float64 array, never start plus step.
- **Spectral axis.** The file's unit or a converted one, through the same plan as the grid labels. The plot reads upward in spectral value: rows are channels, drawn flipped when the axis falls with the channel, with the axis values at the ends; columns are samples.
- **Bounds.** Samples × channels at most 4 Mi cells (16 MiB), samples × cross samples × channels at most 2^28 interpolations, and the cube is read in small boxes: the path is processed in runs of 128 samples and each run reads only its own bounding box over a block of channels from the memory map, so a diagonal across a large cube does not read the whole cube. The op runs on the heavy lane, which is cancelled by killing its process when the browser aborts; the panel has a Cancel button.
- **Display.** Axes labelled, the pane's colour map and stretch with the 1st to 99th percentile of the finite cells, a readout (offset, spectral value, value, how many of the width's samples were averaged) by pointer or by arrow keys on the focused plot, and a statement of how it was made (path, width, interpolation, conversion).
- **Orientation maps.** Refused with 409, for the same reason.

### 3. The line region
A new region shape `line`: vertices (whole pixels, 0-based, y from the bottom), `width` and `widthUnit` (`pixel` or `arcsec`). Saved regions accept it (`internal/regions`, no schema change: the shape is JSON text). It has no area: it is never the working region, never the scene's `active`, and every area consumer refuses it with a sentence (worker `region_mask`, `GET /spectrum`, the axial route, the figure's region). The scene's `regions` overlay carries saved lines (`SceneLine` in the contract, strict Go reading) and draws them as a polyline with a chevron at the end, which is the direction of the offset. A line is drawn with the Line tool (drag; Shift adds a vertex), typed as vertices, or chosen from the saved list; its width is part of the path and is saved with it. DS9: a two-vertex line with a pixel width is written as `# projection(x1,y1,x2,y2,width)` in image coordinates; a polyline or an arcsec width has no exact DS9 shape and is written as a comment, never approximated. Importing lines from DS9 is not built (a DS9 line has no width; choosing one would invent it).

### 4. What a figure stores
A channel-grid figure stores a separate versioned description, `channel-grid/1`, not an extension of `scene/1`. A scene is what one pane draws (one base channel, overlays in that pane's pixels, a view window); a grid is many panels of one dataset with one shared scale. Folding it into `scene/1` as a view variant would make every reader of a scene (`checkScene`, `figureScene`, the pane reducer, the figure's channel and spectral-value checks) handle a base with no single channel. It is stored in the existing `figures.scene` column, told apart by its `schema` key, so there is no migration (this branch started from schema 12; parameter sweeps has since taken 13; this change uses no schema version). The contract says `Figure.scene` is `oneOf [Scene, ChannelGrid]`.
- `internal/scene.ParseChannelGrid` reads it strictly (unknown keys, nulls, non-finite or inverted limits, a range or panel count that disagrees, average above stride, more than 64 panels, panels over the pixel caps, an unknown colour map: twilight is angles only). The server stores its own re-marshalling.
- The figure's view is `kind: channelgrid` with a `grid` block (range, stride, average, previews, unit, convention, where the scale came from) and custom limits in the file's brightness unit. Provenance (reviewer-safe, no dataset id) says that the panels share one scale, how a mean was made, that the panels are previews, and the label unit. `checkChannelGrid` holds the stored description to the view and the dataset (same dataset, same limits, colour map, stretch, range, labels).
- The PNG is composed in the browser from the loaded panels (every panel must be loaded, so the picture shows what the description states), with labels, a colour bar with the limits, and the caption lines.
- A PV diagram is not saved as a figure in this slice. The saved line is the evidence of the cut; a figure of the diagram is deferred.

## Alternatives
- **Extend scene/1 with a `channelGrid` view variant.** Rejected for the reasons above; a second schema string is cheaper than a union in every scene consumer.
- **A new nullable column for the grid.** Cleaner storage, but a schema bump (and a snapshot) for a column that holds the same kind of thing (the description a picture was drawn from).
- **Shared scale from whichever panels have loaded.** That is a scale that changes as the person scrolls. Rejected.
- **`scipy.ndimage.map_coordinates` in the worker.** It turns any NaN neighbour into NaN even at zero weight, adds a dependency and is called once per channel. A vectorised bilinear gather over a block of channels gives control over NaN and is checked against scipy.
- **Offsets as start plus a constant step.** Wrong away from the equator or the tangent point; separations are used.

## Consequences
- New worker module `cube_slices.py` (listed in `services/scientific-worker/DEPLOY`, so remote hosts and the RPM carry it), ops `slab`, `channel_grid` and `pv`; `pv` and `channel_grid` are heavy-lane ops. `convert.py` gains a vectorised core (`spectral_plan`, `convert_spectral_many`).
- Routes: `GET /api/datasets/{id}/channel-grid`, `GET /api/datasets/{id}/slab`, `POST /api/datasets/{id}/pv`, all described in `bodies.json` (`undescribed.txt` did not grow).
- `regions.Shape` and `scene.Region` are no longer comparable with `==` (they hold a slice); the tests that compared them use `reflect.DeepEqual`.
- The web client gets a Channel maps tab on the stage, a Position-velocity inspector section and the Line tool. These screens did not go through the design-approval gate: Stage S waives the mock during this push, so they follow `docs/design/design-system.md` and the existing controls, and the owner reviews them afterwards. Screenshots were looked at (dark, 1440 by 900); light, 1280 and 390 widths were not.

## Validation
- `services/scientific-worker/test_cube_slices.py` (worker through its line protocol): a synthetic cube with a Gaussian line whose centre moves 2 channels per pixel along x gives a PV diagram whose line-centre slope against the offset is 2/60 channels per arcsec and 2 km/s per 60 arcsec; offsets equal astropy separations at 60° declination with half-degree pixels (and differ from pixel count × scale by more than 100 arcsec); width averaging excludes NaN and counts (2 of 3, 0 of 3 gives NaN, not 0); bilinear interpolation equals scipy's and a NaN pixel contaminates only samples that weigh it; nearest never mixes; a polyline follows its vertices; an arcsec width becomes whole pixels with the note; without a celestial WCS offsets are pixels and an arcsec width is refused; caps and malformed requests are refused before any read; samples outside the image hold no value; the axis equals the existing `convert` op's value for the same channel; a slab mean excludes NaN, counts them and its preview is the block mean of the slab mean; panel layout, labels against `convert` and `coord`, the pooled percentile scale and custom limits, panel and pixel caps, a refused conversion keeping native labels.
- Go: `internal/regions` (line round trip, bounds, DS9 projection and comment), `internal/scene` (shared fixtures with a saved line accepted and six malformed ones refused; `channel-grid/1` strict reading and caps), `internal/server` (the three routes with the real worker against the described schemas, the cache, 409 for an orientation map, caps and bad requests; figure save with a grid refused when it disagrees with its view, stored as the server read it, provenance reviewer-safe; a saved line inside a figure's scene).
- Web (vitest): panel labels, one scale for every panel (same value, same colour), hatched no-value pixels, captions that state scale, averaging, preview and what is not drawn, the stored description against the view; the lazy panel reads, the request the controls send, saving only when every panel is loaded; the PV frame, pointer and keyboard readout, cancel, the width kept with the path; saved lines selected into the path and never the working region.
- `e2e/tests/yy-channelmaps.spec.ts` opens the grid (labels, stated scale, mean of two channels in km/s, figure saved with its description, axe) and draws a line, computes the diagram, reads a cell with the keyboard, saves the line, checks the DS9 projection and that a spectrum of a line is refused.
- Not verified: a real survey cube (the fixtures are 32 × 24 × 64); timings of a large PV against the caps; the grid and diagram at 1280 and 390 widths and in light mode.

## Rollback / Migration
No migration. A figure of kind `channelgrid` and a saved line are ignored by older code only if it tolerates unknown kinds: an older server would refuse a scene with a saved line (it reads only rect and circle), so saved lines should be deleted before rolling back. Removing the three routes and `cube_slices.py` leaves everything else working.
